# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** The consumer Mara, a shift worker with two open claims and variable but stable income
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Agree an instalment plan she can actually afford and keep, without making her situation worse.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** She opens the portal after a reminder and hits the income and affordability questions. She doesn't know why they're asked, what an affordable plan looks like, whether a wrong answer or a missed payment will make things worse, or whether both claims can be handled together. Unable to find the right documents or get answers, she gives up and calls an agent. This also fits your "panic" angle: the fear of consequences is the trigger.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** The phone (a call to the agent line) is the only external channel the brief names, plus her own phone camera or files to produce income documents.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Opens the portal from the reminder and starts the instalment journey.
Hits the income questions and tries to work out and document a "typical month" from variable shift pay (inferred).
Searches for payslips or statements she can't easily find, and uploads what she has (partly inferred; the brief says documents are hard to find).
Gives up on the portal and calls an agent.
Repeats identity, affordability and case details to the agent, and asks the questions the portal never answered, including whether both claims can be handled together.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The moment she reaches the income and affordability questions and can't tell why they're asked, what an affordable plan looks like, or whether a wrong answer or missed payment will make things worse. Her next move is to call. The second broken moment is on the phone, when the agent has no record of what she already entered and she starts again from zero.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** The evidence available is behavioral:

Research theme: "Consumers don't know what an affordable agreement looks like," "don't understand why specific information is requested," and "fear the consequences of giving a wrong answer or missing a payment."
Research theme: "Agents receive too little context when a digital journey is escalated to them."
Baseline data: 31% of journey starters contact an agent within 7 days, and 28% of uploaded income documents need clarification or review.
