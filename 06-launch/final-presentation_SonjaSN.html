Slide 5 · Strategy

Problem hook & value proposition

Problem hook

We must solve the rising cost & weak instalment journey by addressing the user's uncertainties & possibly panic situation

Strategic crisis · 12 months of doing nothing

If we do nothing for 12 months, the company will have increased agent costs & fall behind the strategic objective of 80% self-service

Moment of misery

The user is currently forced to guess & reach out to the agent to get support for the instalment

Cold read

High stake risk: it touches cost (agent capacity) and strategic portfolio value (company plans that might fail). A systemic problem, not an edge case.

Value proposition

For our consumers, we will offer a guided portal journey because we are losing most starters today right at the beginning of user journey, to align our product priorities with our north-star strategic goal of 80% digital self-service Europewide.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on she doesn't know why they're asked, what an affordable plan looks like, whether a wrong answer or a missed payment will make things worse, or whether both claims can be handled together and 54% of started journeys not ending in an agreement; 31% of journey starters contacting an agent within 7 days; 28% of uploaded income documents needing clarification or review, I believe that adding “Why we ask” to every income question for Mara will result in she doesn't give up and doesn't call an agent, as measured by a -5% change in the share of journey starters who contact an agent within 7 days (baseline 31%, pilot segment against a comparison group). I will protect 60-day plan survival: no more than 19% of new agreements missing a payment within 60 days and will make a go/no-go decision after 4 weeks.

Slide 6 · Research

Competitive analysis & journey map

Persona · workaround

Mara, a shift worker with two open claims and variable but stable income. Goal: agree an instalment plan she can actually afford and keep, without making her situation worse.

External tools

The phone (a call to the agent line) is the only external channel the brief names, plus her own phone camera or files to produce income documents.

The process
  1. Opens the portal from the reminder and starts the instalment journey.
  2. Hits the income questions and tries to work out and document a “typical month” from variable shift pay (inferred).
  3. Searches for payslips or statements she can't easily find, and uploads what she has (partly inferred; the brief says documents are hard to find).
  4. Gives up on the portal and calls an agent.
  5. Repeats identity, affordability and case details to the agent, and asks the questions the portal never answered, including whether both claims can be handled together.
Core frustration

The moment she reaches the income and affordability questions and can't tell why they're asked, what an affordable plan looks like, or whether a wrong answer or missed payment will make things worse. Her next move is to call. The second broken moment is on the phone, when the agent has no record of what she already entered and she starts again from zero.

The evidence · behavioral
  • Research theme: “Consumers don't know what an affordable agreement looks like,” “don't understand why specific information is requested,” and “fear the consequences of giving a wrong answer or missing a payment.”
  • Research theme: “Agents receive too little context when a digital journey is escalated to them.”
  • Baseline data: 31% of journey starters contact an agent within 7 days, and 28% of uploaded income documents need clarification or review.

Feature 1: Single view of both claims (with joint arrangement)

Feature scoped

“Why we ask” on every income question (addresses research theme that consumers don't understand why information is requested)

Must have · after overriding the AI
  1. Each note answers three things: why it's needed, how the answer is used, and who can see it. Dropping any one leaves part of her doubt open. “How used” must state plainly whether the answer feeds a recommendation, in line with the explainability guardrail.
  2. The note sits inline, under the question, with no tap needed. She is on a phone with little time, and a hidden note is one she won't open.
  3. Readable on a small screen and reachable by screen readers. A note she can't read or hear fails her.
Demoted to Should
  • Plain German, short, no legal wording. Should, because even in Germany we're international.
  • Every claim in the note is true and signed off by legal and data-protection. Should, because it would need lots of efforts getting all signs together which would make it not a quick-win anymore.
What my PRD makes explicit

It makes explicit that the note's content is a compliance-gated deliverable, not just interface copy.

Prototype revealed a PRD gap

The PRD says a field without an approved note must not render, but never says what happens to the journey then (skip, block, or route to an agent), so the prototype made the question a dead end with Continue disabled and only the agent link left, which could strand Mara on any question whose copy fails review.

Slide 8 · Validation

A/B experiment plan

Hypothesis

I believe that “Why we ask” on every income question for Mara will result in she doesn't give up and doesn't call an agent, as measured by a -5% change in the share of journey starters who contact an agent within 7 days, within 4 weeks. I will protect 60-day plan survival throughout the test.

Control (A)

31% of starters contact an agent within 7 days, roughly 2,600. The research themes say consumers don't know what's affordable, don't understand why income is requested, and fear the consequences of a wrong answer or missed payment. 28% of uploaded income documents need clarification or review.

Variant (B) · summary of the PRD

3 screens: entry point, income and affordability questions, success / confirmation. Under each of the 4 income fields, an always-visible note with Why / How used / Who sees lines. Notes of 40 words or fewer (proposed), all copy from one content object, de-DE only, no outcome promises.

Primary metric

Share of journey starters contacting an agent within 7 days

Baseline31%
MDE-5%
Sample / arm2,796
Split · duration50/50 · 4 wk
Significance0.05
Guardrail

60-day plan survival: no more than 19% of new agreements missing a payment within 60 days (baseline), so a higher completion rate can't come from plans people can't keep. Must not fall below 81%. Today, 19% miss a payment. No room below that baseline.

Ship

If the primary metric improves by at least -5% at 0.05 and 60-day plan survival does not fall below 81%, after 4 weeks.

Iterate

If direction is positive but lift is below the MDE.

Kill

If the primary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively.

The read date is fixed at the end of 4 weeks. No results reviewed before this date.

Slide 9 · Launch

GTM launch plan

Goal

Engagement. Encourage the users to add the information we ask for.

Audience

Mara, a shift worker with two open claims and variable but stable income. Goal: agree an instalment plan she can actually afford and keep, without making her situation worse.

Launch tier

S · Minimal We're only adjusting content in an already existing feature.

Channels
Owned: Consumer Portal Owned: E-Mail with deeplink to the Portal Owned: Letter
Enablement & assets

Short demo of how to set up an instalment that matches your income or add info in any of the existing FAQs.

Ownership & budget
  • Plan step, sentence, and rule: the product manager of this PRD, one person.
  • Build in the portal: one engineering lead, one person.
  • One-pager for the call: one person in collections who briefs Daniel. The case study names no individuals.
  • No media spend. The extra cost is the briefing and the measurement of the call within 7 days and the keep rate at 60 days. Neither is visible today.
  • Do not build: paid campaigns, influencers, a model, an image check, reminders, or a launch outside the Germany pilot.
Timeline
  • Phase 1, beta: 50/50 in the one segment, 14 days of enrollment plus 7 days of follow-up. Do not ship on day 21.
  • Phase 2, launch: only once, 60 days after agreement, the keep rate in the variant is at least 81% and the call rate has fallen by at least 5 percentage points below 31%. Then in the portal only, for this segment.
  • Phase 3, after launch: keep reading the keep rate, with the one-pager in use. No further market while the 81% holds.
Success metrics

Share of starters who contact an agent within 7 days.

Warning sign: the rate falls and the 60-day keep rate drops below 81%.

Share who see the comfortable instalment and the sentence before they confirm.

Warning sign: confirmations rise while the sentence was not shown.

Share of new agreements kept through 60 days, floor 81%.

Warning sign: the miss rate rises above 19%.

Bad signal to watch for

Many people see the instalment, and the call rate stays at 31%. Or calls fall and the 60-day miss rate rises. Then she agreed without being able to keep the instalment.

Likely post-launch decision

Repeat. Trigger: the call rate falls by less than 5 percentage points, or it falls and the keep rate is below 81%. Double only if both are true: at least 5 percentage points fewer calls and a keep rate of at least 81%. Lower the priority if the call rate does not fall or it rises.
